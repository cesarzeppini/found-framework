## The one-liner `[ask]`

## Messaging `[ask]`

The two or three things we say consistently. Each with a sentence on why it matters to the reader.

## What we claim, and what we don't `[ask]`

Claims we can support. Then the ones we deliberately avoid — competitor framing we don't adopt, categories we don't put ourselves in, outcomes we won't promise.

voice-guard checks copy against this. Without the second list it can only catch banned words, not borrowed positioning.

## Tone of voice `[ask]`

**Base rules.** Three to five, each concrete enough to act on. "Plain, specific, never breathless" beats "friendly and professional".

**Register.** Formality, use of contractions, first person singular or plural, whether we address the reader as "you".

**Sentence patterns.** What our sentences do: length, whether we open on a benefit or a fact, whether questions are used, whether the product is ever the subject. These are what make copy recognisable — a post can clear every banned word and still sound like nobody.

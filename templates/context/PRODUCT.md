# Product

What we're building and for whom.

## Purpose `[ask]`

What this product is for, in a few sentences. The problem it solves and for whom. Written so someone who has never seen it understands what it does before they understand how.

## Users `[ask]`

Who uses this. Their context, what they're trying to get done, what they know and don't. Where there are distinct groups — admin and member, buyer and seller — name each and what separates them.

This section is what Spec derives permissions and edge cases from, and what Ship's channel agents write to.

## Personality `[ask]`

How the product should feel to use: calm, dense, playful, utilitarian. One or two adjectives with a sentence each on what they mean in practice.

Personality here is about *behaviour* — pacing, how much it asks of the user, how it handles mistakes. The visual expression is DESIGN.md's and the written expression is BRAND.md's.

## What it is not `[ask]`

The adjacent products this is not trying to be. Prevents scope drift at spec time, and stops collateral positioning it as something it isn't.

## Current shape `[derive]`

The main surfaces that exist today — routes, key screens, the core objects a user works with. Derived from the codebase; a map for spec work, not a specification.
